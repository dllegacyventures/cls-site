The story: from my childhood bedroom to systems

I don't tell this story because it's especially heroic. I tell it because it explains why I work the way I do today: I don't trust trends anymore, only processes you can repeat.

  1. The beginningAn ice cream café that others destroyed

    My parents came to Germany from South America. My father barely spoke German and still built up an ice cream café. Other people wrecked it out of envy. After that, both of my parents worked full-time. That's when I knew I wanted to be the one who brings the money home.

  2. At 18Four attempts, four times nothing

    Dropshipping, trading, memecoins, my own fashion brand. Months of work every time, nothing to show for it every time. In the end I was sitting in my childhood bedroom with five-figure debt. What I learned from it is in the journal: Four failed businesses at 18.

  3. The turnSystems instead of tactics

    I wasn't short on effort, and I didn't need the next trend. What I was missing was a system that works without luck. So I stopped chasing hype and started building processes.

  4. The momentThe first clone

    I watched an AI build a website in minutes. My thought: this isn't a tool, it's an employee. I built my first clone, offered it to businesses and landed my first projects.

  5. TodayBuilding so my father can stop

    I build AI systems for my own companies and for businesses. My father is over 60 and still working. I build so he can stop. That's the most honest reason I have.

Principles

I'm not a guru and I don't want to be one. The words I use to describe myself are simpler: contrarian thinker, founder, systems builder, son. What that means day to day:

  • Output over theory. I show what runs. Anything I haven't built or measured myself, I call an estimate.
  • No income promises. Not on this site and not in CLS. What you control is the work, the conversations and the execution, not the number in your bank account.
  • In sales, emotion beats systems. My scripts are deliberately linear, one thread from A to Z, no flowcharts. The conversation stays with the human. AI handles preparation, follow-up and analysis. More on that: Why emotion beats systems.
  • Systems over luck. A process that works once has to work a second time without me standing next to it. Otherwise it isn't a system.
